# Ordinary modern English that /usr/share/dict/words predates.
#
# WHY THIS EXISTS. The validator flags any word of six or more letters that is
# neither ordinary English nor known medical vocabulary, which is the check
# that catches "pipercellum" for piperacillin. Its English reference is web2,
# a dictionary compiled in 1934. It has no "website", no "workflow", no
# "anymore" — so dictating an ordinary email raised a clinical review dialog
# on words like "anymore", and roughly a third of common modern vocabulary
# tripped it.
#
# A review nobody reads is worse than no review: it trains clicking through
# the same dialog that guards mangled drug names.
#
# WHY A LIST RATHER THAN A RULE. Nearly every word here is a closed compound
# ("any" + "more"), so splitting compounds and checking both halves looks like
# the elegant fix. It was prototyped and rejected: it also unflagged real
# mis-transcriptions, because "sifteraline" splits into "sifter" + "aline" and
# "septipine" into "septi" + "pine". That is sertraline and cefepime going
# silently unflagged — a 13% leak rate on exactly the words this check exists
# to catch. An explicit list cannot leak; only what is listed passes.
#
# SCOPE. General and technical English only. Clinical vocabulary belongs in
# general_medicine.txt or infectious_diseases.txt, and names of people and
# institutions belong in institutions_and_people.local.txt, which is
# gitignored. Nothing here may be a plausible garbled drug or organism name.
#
# This file is committed: it contains no clinical judgement and nothing
# identifying, just English the 1930s had not invented yet.
#
# One term per line. Case-insensitive.
